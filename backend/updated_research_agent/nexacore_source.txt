SECTION 1: CORPORATE OVERVIEW AND BUSINESS DESCRIPTION
NexaCore Technologies Limited is an Indian multinational information technology services and consulting corporation incorporated on September 14, 1994. Headquartered in Hyderabad, Telangana, the Company operates across 42 countries and serves clients in 58 nations across Banking and Financial Services, Manufacturing, Retail, Communication, Life Sciences, and Energy verticals. As of March 31, 2025, NexaCore employed 1,91,204 people globally and reported consolidated revenue of Rs 98,450 crore, making it one of India's largest IT services companies by revenue.
The Company operates through five primary service lines: Application Development and Maintenance, which contributed 34.2% of consolidated revenue in FY2025; Cloud Infrastructure and Managed Services at 23.1%; AI and Data Analytics through its proprietary ARIA platform at 11.3%; Business Process Management at 10.0%; and Engineering and Product Development Services at 10.6%.

SECTION 2: MANAGEMENT DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS
Global IT services revenue growth moderated to approximately 5.8% in CY2024 as enterprise discretionary spending remained cautious, particularly in North America and Europe.
NexaCore's consolidated revenue grew 7.3% in reported rupee terms and 5.1% in constant currency for FY2025, within the Company's revised guidance range of 5%-7% in constant currency. However, this falls short of the 8%-10% constant currency growth originally guided at the start of the year. The revenue shortfall was concentrated in the BFSI vertical in North America during the first half of FY2025, where large banking clients deferred discretionary programs, delaying an estimated Rs 2,100 crore of revenue from H1 into H2. Recovery in H2 was partial.
Revenue by Industry Vertical FY2025: BFSI 30,716 cr (+4.6%, CC +2.8%). Retail & Consumer 14,768 cr (+7.3%, CC +5.2%). Manufacturing 14,276 cr (+11.1%, CC +9.2%). Communication & Media 9,254 cr (-5.8%, CC -7.1%). Life Sciences & Healthcare 8,878 cr (+12.4%, CC +10.6%). Energy, Utilities & Resources 8,387 cr (+11.4%, CC +9.7%). Hi-Tech & Others 12,171 cr (+15.5%, CC +13.8%).
Communication & Media declined 7.1% in constant currency for the second consecutive year; large deal TCV fell 66.7% to USD 80 mn; strategic review including potential divestment is underway.
The BFSI vertical, at 31.2% of revenue, represents the Company's largest single vertical. One significant BFSI client generating approximately Rs 2,840 crore of annual revenue has communicated a potential 15%-20% reduction in technology spending for FY2026, representing a revenue risk of Rs 426-568 crore.
Consolidated operating margin (EBIT margin) for FY2025 was 19.8%, compared to 21.4% in FY2024, a compression of 160 basis points. This marks the fourth consecutive year of operating margin decline from the peak of 24.6% in FY2022.
Operating Margin Bridge from FY2024 21.4% to FY2025 19.8%: Revenue mix shift to lower-margin segments -70 bps. Wage inflation (effective April 2024) -110 bps. Subcontracting and third-party cost increases -50 bps. Currency impact (INR appreciation) -30 bps. Fresher hiring optimization savings +40 bps. Automation and delivery pyramid rationalization +30 bps. Facility and travel cost reductions +20 bps.
Employee costs (+10.1%) and subcontracting costs (+23.9% restated) both grew materially faster than revenue (+7.3%), driving the 160 bps margin compression.
Exceptional item: Rs 412 crore, Reichmann Automotive GmbH SAP S/4HANA delivery dispute settlement (EUR 22 mn cash + EUR 22 mn remediation services); recognised Q3 FY2025; no insurance recovery.

SECTION 7: RISK FACTORS, AUDITOR'S REPORT AND GOVERNANCE
Revenue Concentration Risk: North America represents 56.0% of consolidated revenue. Top 20 clients contribute 54.8% of revenue. One BFSI client generating Rs 2,840 crore annually has indicated a potential 15%-20% spending reduction for FY2026.
Operating Margin Risk: Operating margin has compressed 480 basis points from its FY2022 peak. Margin recovery in FY2026 depends on fresher hiring (18,000-22,000 planned), subcontractor reduction (4,200 roles), and a wage freeze for the broader workforce.
Talent and Attrition Risk: Voluntary attrition increased from 14.9% in FY2024 to 17.4% in FY2025. Attrition in AI and cloud skill areas was between 22.8% and 26.4%. Compensation for AI and cloud roles is estimated to be 12%-18% below market median. Average employee tenure declined from 4.6 years to 4.2 years.
Goodwill Impairment Risk: The Company carries goodwill of Rs 7,640 crore, entirely allocated to the AI and Data Analytics CGU following the Klar Analytics GmbH acquisition (December 2023, EUR 284 million). The annual impairment test uses a terminal growth rate of 5.8%, above the industry benchmark range of 2.5%-4.0%, with headroom of only Rs 820 crore. A reduction in the terminal growth rate to 3.5% would reduce headroom to Rs 180 crore; at 2.5%, an impairment charge of approximately Rs 280 crore would be triggered. 18 of the 42 designated key employees at Klar Analytics (42.9%) have resigned since acquisition, including the CTO in October 2024.
Legal and Regulatory Risk: Transfer pricing disputes of Rs 2,140 crore are pending before the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal. A US Department of Justice Civil Investigative Demand was received by NexaCore Inc. on April 11, 2025 regarding H-1B visa practices for FY2021-FY2023. Potential outcomes include civil penalties and, in severe scenarios, debarment from the H-1B program, affecting 56% of revenue. Baker McKenzie LLP retained as external counsel. No provision made.
Subsidiary and Audit Risk: Three subsidiaries representing 15.3% of consolidated revenue were unaudited at the filing date. NexaCore Digital Inc. is expected to receive a going concern qualification. NexaCore BPM Services Limited has Rs 100 crore of trade receivables that remained unreconciled following an ERP migration.
Independent Auditor's Report: Auditor S.R. Khandelwal & Associates LLP. Audit Opinion Unmodified. Two Emphasis of Matter paragraphs: the Rs 412 crore Reichmann exceptional charge, and the DoJ Civil Investigative Demand.
Key Audit Matters: Revenue Recognition on Fixed-Price Contracts, 54% of revenue, systematic cost underestimation in Engineering Services averaging 8.4% of contract value, 38 of 84 sampled contracts (45.2%) lacked independent review, a Significant Deficiency was identified. Goodwill Impairment Assessment, headroom Rs 820 crore, terminal growth rate 5.8% at aggressive end of 2.5%-4.5% benchmark range. Transfer Pricing Disputes, Rs 156 crore settled implying some technical merit in the tax department's position. NexaCore Digital Inc DTA Recoverability, Rs 84 crore deferred tax asset assessed as a "close judgment" given two consecutive years of missed forecasts.
Auditor's Observation on Consistency: The CEO's letter to shareholders states free cash flow of Rs 12,840 crore. The audited Statement of Cash Flows yields FCF of Rs 9,062 crore under the standard definition (operating cash flow less capex). The auditor requested management provide a prominent reconciliation of these two figures in future communications.
Internal Financial Controls Significant Deficiencies: (1) Revenue recognition cost-to-complete estimation in Engineering Services without independent review in 45.2% of sampled contracts. (2) Six vendors received payments before completion of mandatory background verification.
Corporate Governance: CEO Total Remuneration Rs 41.84 cr in FY2025 vs Rs 38.40 cr FY2024, growth +8.9% vs PAT growth +0.8%. CEO-to-median employee pay ratio 53.4x vs 52.1x vs 48.4x over three years. Variable Pay Approved 72% of maximum in FY2025 despite the CEO missing revenue growth, PAT growth, and DSO improvement targets. Ethics Hotline Complaints 284 vs 248 vs 192, substantiated 16.9%. One insider trading window breach self-reported to SEBI in FY2025.
