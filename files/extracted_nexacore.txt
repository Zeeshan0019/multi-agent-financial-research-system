SECTION 1: CORPORATE OVERVIEW AND BUSINESS DESCRIPTION 
NexaCore Technologies Limited is an Indian multinational information technology services and consulting 
corporation incorporated on September 14, 1994. Headquartered in Hyderabad, Telangana, the Company operates 
across 42 countries and serves clients in 58 nations across Banking and Financial Services, Manufacturing, Retail, 
Communication, Life Sciences, and Energy verticals. As of March 31, 2025, NexaCore employed 1,91,204 people 
globally and reported consolidated revenue of ₹98,450 crore, making it one of India's largest IT services companies 
by revenue. 
The Company operates through five primary service lines: Application Development and Maintenance, which 
contributed 34.2% of consolidated revenue in FY2025; Cloud Infrastructure and Managed Services at 23.1%; AI 
and Data Analytics through its proprietary ARIA platform at 11.3%; Business Process Management at 10.0%; and 
Engineering and Product Development Services at 10.6%. The remaining 10.8% comprises other services and 
unallocated revenue.

SECTION 2: MANAGEMENT DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS 
Industry Context and Business Environment 
Global IT services revenue growth moderated to approximately 5.8% in CY2024 as enterprise discretionary 
spending remained cautious, particularly in North America and Europe. 
NexaCore's consolidated revenue grew 7.3% in reported rupee terms and 5.1% in constant currency for FY2025, 
within the Company's revised guidance range of 5%–7% in constant currency. However, this falls short of the 8%–
10% constant currency growth originally guided at the start of the year. The revenue shortfall was concentrated in 
the BFSI vertical in North America during the first half of FY2025, where large banking clients deferred 
discretionary programs, delaying an estimated ₹2,100 crore of revenue from H1 into H2. Recovery in H2 was 
partial.

 SECTION 7: RISK FACTORS, AUDITOR'S REPORT AND GOVERNANCE 
A. Key Risk Factors 
1. Revenue Concentration Risk: North America represents 56.0% of consolidated revenue. The Company's top 20 
clients contribute 54.8% of revenue. A slowdown in US enterprise technology spending could materially impact 
revenue growth. One BFSI client generating ₹2,840 crore annually has indicated a potential 15%–20% spending 
reduction for FY2026. 
2. Operating Margin Risk: Operating margin has compressed 480 basis points from its FY2022 peak. Employee 
costs and subcontracting costs are both growing faster than revenue. The planned margin recovery in FY2026 
depends on successful execution of fresher hiring (18,000–22,000 planned), subcontractor reduction (4,200 roles), 
and a wage freeze for the broader workforce — all subject to execution uncertainty in a high-attrition environment. 
3. Talent and Attrition Risk: V oluntary attrition increased from 14.9% in FY2024 to 17.4% in FY2025. Attrition 
in AI and cloud skill areas was between 22.8% and 26.4%. The Company's compensation for AI and cloud roles is 
estimated to be 12%–18% below market median. No overall wage increase is planned for the broader FY2026 
workforce. Average employee tenure declined from 4.6 years to 4.2 years. 
4. Goodwill Impairment Risk: The Company carries goodwill of ₹7,640 crore, entirely allocated to the AI and 
Data Analytics CGU following the Klar Analytics GmbH acquisition (December 2023, EUR 284 million). The 
annual impairment test uses a terminal growth rate of 5.8% — above the industry benchmark range of 2.5%–4.0% 
— with a headroom of only ₹820 crore. A reduction in the terminal growth rate to 3.5% would reduce headroom to 
₹180 crore; at 2.5%, an impairment charge of approximately ₹280 crore would be triggered. Additionally, 18 of the 
42 designated key employees at Klar Analytics (42.9%) have resigned since acquisition, including the Chief 
Technology Officer in October 2024. 
5. Legal and Regulatory Risk: Transfer pricing disputes of ₹2,140 crore are pending before the Income Tax 
Appellate Tribunal. A US Department of Justice Civil Investigative Demand was received by NexaCore Inc. on 
April 11, 2025 regarding H-1B visa practices for FY2021–FY2023. Potential outcomes include civil penalties and, 
in severe scenarios, debarment from the H-1B program — which would significantly impair the ability to deploy 
=== PAGE 12 ===
talent to US client sites, affecting 56% of revenue. The Company retained Baker McKenzie LLP as external 
counsel. No provision has been made as the outcome cannot be estimated. 
6. Subsidiary and Audit Risk: Three subsidiaries representing 15.3% of consolidated revenue were unaudited at 
the filing date of this Annual Report. NexaCore Digital Inc. is expected to receive a going concern qualification 
from its Canadian auditor. NexaCore BPM Services Limited has ₹100 crore of trade receivables that remained 
unreconciled following an ERP migration.
